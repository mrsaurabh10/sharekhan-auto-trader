# Stop/target and entry rejection investigation — 7 October 2026

## Confirmed index feed defect

The server's Sharekhan script master identifies NIFTY as NC / 20000 / NIFTY. Its Shoonya master stores NSE / 26000 / NIFTY INDEX. Bank Nifty is stored as NSE / 26009 / NIFTY BANK.

The existing Shoonya resolver treats these spot scripts as cash equities: it looks up NIFTY and then NIFTY-EQ, or BANKNIFTY and then BANKNIFTY-EQ. Those rows do not exist on the server. The periodic quote poller receives an empty instrument resolution and skips the index. This is a concrete defect in the primary spot refresh path.

Indicators use separately fetched five-minute candles. Exits use cached live spot prices and, for spot stops, a completed minute candle plus a current price still beyond the stop. A working candle history does not establish that live stop/target monitoring receives current ticks. If the index cache is populated only by an earlier WebSocket price, the broken poller does not refresh it; the exit monitor can keep using that old price. If no completed post-entry minute exists in the cache, the spot stop cannot be confirmed.

These spot levels are monitored by the application, rather than being native broker-held stop orders. Simulator execution does not remove the requirement for working price monitoring.

Retained Docker log rotation no longer covers the trade's morning interval. The precise cached tick sequence and contemporaneous monitor callbacks cannot be reconstructed, so the defect is confirmed but the exact chain of events for every missed crossing cannot be proved.

## Why CE entries were rejected

After indicator qualification, TradeExecutionService.isEntrySignalStillValid performs a second price check immediately before placing the order:

- CE: cached current spot must be greater than or equal to the signal candle close.
- PE: cached current spot must be less than or equal to the signal candle close.

The 10:25 CE signal close was 22697.90; therefore a cached spot below 22697.90 rejected requests 12954/12955. The 14:25 signal close was 22639.85; a cached spot below that level rejected requests 12966/12967. Both rejections were generated by application validation, before an executed CE position existed. A genuine price pullback can trigger this rule even though the completed candle passed all indicator conditions. A stale spot cache can also trigger it incorrectly.

The old rejection audit preserved option quote information but not the current spot or its observation time. Those exact spot values cannot now be supplied. The 10:30–10:35 spot bar also closed well below the signal close, demonstrating a pullback during that interval, but it does not establish the exact price at rejection time.

## Local corrections

1. Resolve NIFTY and Bank Nifty spot scripts by their known NSE index tokens in the persisted Shoonya master.
2. Accept recognized label variants for those two index tokens, including NIFTY INDEX versus Nifty 50. Both the token and the index family must match. Option identities still require the correct contract and token; an index quote cannot pass as an option quote.
3. Preserve the rejection's current spot price, signal close and spot observation time in the database audit and application log.

The second entry price check itself is unchanged. Relaxing it to permit entries after a pullback would be a strategy behavior change, distinct from repairing the stale feed.
